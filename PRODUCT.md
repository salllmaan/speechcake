# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Special education professionals: teachers, SLPs, OTs, BCBAs, paraprofessionals, and school district administrators. They work across classrooms, therapy rooms, and on the go, juggling IEP goals, data collection, service minutes, accommodations, behavior tracking, progress reports, and Medicaid billing. The page is hit by individual practitioners researching tools and by district-level decision makers evaluating fit for their teams.

## Product Purpose

AbleSpace is a real-time IEP and special-education workflow platform. The landing page exists to convert two audiences:

1. Practitioners who need to feel that AbleSpace understands the specifics of their work (goal tracking, defensible documentation, service minutes, collaboration with paras) and saves them hours every week.
2. District buyers who need to see compliance (HIPAA, FERPA, ISO 27001), data security, role-based access, and team-scale collaboration.

Success looks like booking demos and free signups from both audiences while signaling clinical credibility and warmth simultaneously.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, calm, capable. The voice is confident without being slick, warm without being childish, expert without being clinical. AbleSpace knows the field deeply and respects the practitioner's expertise. It does not condescend, gamify, or oversell. The emotional goal is relief: the reader should feel "finally, a tool built by people who get this work."

## Anti-references

This page must NOT look like:

- **Generic AI / SaaS templates.** Cream backgrounds, purple gradients, abstract blob illustrations, the hero-metric template (big number, small label, gradient accent). The default training-data reflex for any AI product. Reject.
- **Sterile medical / clinical interfaces.** Navy and teal, hospital-grade sans-serif, cold institutional surfaces. AbleSpace serves clinicians but is not a hospital EMR.
- **EdTech cartoony.** Rainbow gradients, illustrated mascots, oversized rounded shapes, primary-color confetti. This is professional software for adults, not a K-12 student app.
- **Crypto / neon-on-black.** Dark mode flexing, neon accents, terminal aesthetics. Wrong audience and tone entirely.

## Design Principles

1. **Field-specific over generic.** Every section should feel built specifically for special-education work in language, screenshots, and examples. Generic "boost productivity" framing fails this brand.
2. **Calm credibility over loud claims.** Restraint reads as expertise; aggressive marketing reads as unserious. Favor compliance badges, real product UI, and quiet confidence over hype.
3. **Practitioner respect.** Treat readers as domain experts who already know what an IEP is, what service minutes are, what FERPA requires. Do not over-explain or over-decorate.
4. **Show the product, not metaphors.** Real screenshots, real workflows, real numbers, not abstract illustrations of "AI helping you."
5. **Warmth through humans, not whimsy.** Warmth comes from copy, testimonials, and photography of actual practitioners, not bright colors or playful illustration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA minimum for color contrast, focus states, and interactive targets.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Animations are decorative, never required for comprehension.
- Color is never the sole signal for status, state, or category. Pair with text, icon, or shape.
- Avoid red and green as the sole differentiator for color-blindness safety.
- Keyboard navigation works for every interactive element; tab order matches visual order.
- Image alt text is descriptive and accurate, not decorative-by-default.
